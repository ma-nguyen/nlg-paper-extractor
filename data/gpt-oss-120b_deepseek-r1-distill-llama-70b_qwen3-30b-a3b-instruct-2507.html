1. On the Blind Spots of Model-Based Evaluation Metrics for Text Generation
gpt-oss-120b
goal

To investigate the robustness of recent pretrained‑language‑model‑based evaluation metrics for text generation by designing synthetic stress‑test datasets that introduce controlled errors, and to analyze how these metrics respond to such perturbations across open‑ended generation, translation, and summarization tasks.

findings

The study uncovers systematic blind spots in many PLM‑based metrics: BERTScore is misled by truncation errors in summarization; MAUVE (especially with GPT‑2 features) is insensitive to errors at the beginning or middle of texts, while MAUVE‑ELECTRA is more robust; UniEval can be tricked by valueless injected instruction text; GPT‑PPL and MLM‑PPL unfairly favor sequences composed of frequent n‑grams; log‑probability metrics exhibit a self‑evaluation bias, rewarding outputs generated by the same underlying PLM used for evaluation; several metrics (e.g., BARTScore, BERTScore, ROUGE, COMET, PRISM, UniEval) fail truncation tests, and some (BARTScore‑para‑recall, MAUVE‑RoBERTa) fail sentence‑switching tests. The paper also provides analyses of why these failures occur (e.g., attention patterns, precision/recall trade‑offs) and suggests practical workarounds such as using alternative PLM features (e.g., RoBERTa/ELECTRA for MAUVE), reporting multiple score variants, calibrating f‑measure, and combining complementary metrics to mitigate individual blind spots.

2. Finding Blind Spots in Evaluator LLMs with Interpretable Checklists
gpt-oss-120b
goal

To introduce FBI, a meta‑evaluation framework that uses interpretable checklists and targeted perturbations to rigorously assess how well large language models (Evaluator LLMs) can evaluate the four core abilities of other LLMs—factual accuracy, instruction following, long‑form coherence, and reasoning—and to benchmark their performance across single‑answer, pairwise, and reference‑guided evaluation strategies.

findings

Evaluator LLMs exhibit substantial blind spots: across 2400 perturbed responses in 22 categories, they missed quality degradations in more than 50% of cases on average. Single‑answer and pairwise setups performed poorly, while reference‑guided evaluations were comparatively better but still error‑prone. GPT‑4‑Turbo, the strongest evaluator, failed to consistently penalize perturbed answers except on reasoning tasks, and added rubrics or axes did not reliably improve detection. Other popular evaluators (Gemini‑1.5‑Pro, Claude‑3‑Opus, LLaMA‑3‑70B‑Instruct, Prometheus 2) showed similar or worse shortcomings. Explanations occasionally identified errors but rarely led to appropriate score changes. Expanding the scoring range from 1‑3 to 1‑5 offered only modest gains. Overall, current Evaluator LLMs are unreliable for automated assessment of LLM‑generated text, especially in reference‑free scenarios.

3. Order in the Evaluation Court: A Critical Analysis of NLG Evaluation Trends
gpt-oss-120b
goal

To systematically analyze how evaluation practices for Natural Language Generation have evolved over the past six years by automatically extracting and quantifying metadata from 14,171 NLG papers, focusing on the use of automatic metrics, LLM-as-a-judge (LaaJ) methods, and human evaluations.

findings

The study uncovered three critical trends: (1) Task divergence – Dialogue Generation has rapidly shifted toward LaaJ (>40% in 2025) while Machine Translation remains dominated by n‑gram metrics and Question Answering shows a marked decline in human evaluation; (2) Metric inertia – generic metrics such as BLEU and ROUGE continue to be widely applied across tasks despite the availability of more semantically aware alternatives and without empirical justification; (3) Human‑LaaJ divergence – LaaJ and human evaluations prioritize different quality signals, explicit validation of LaaJ against humans is rare (<8% of papers) and correlations are only moderate to low, indicating that LLM judges are not reliable proxies for human judgment.

4. Unveiling the Achilles' Heel of NLG Evaluators: A Unified Adversarial Framework Driven by Large Language Models
gpt-oss-120b
goal

To introduce AdvEval, a novel black-box adversarial framework that leverages strong large language models as both data generators and gold evaluators to automatically create adversarial NLG examples that cause strong disagreement between human judgments (proxied by the gold LLM) and existing automatic NLG evaluators, thereby probing the robustness of these evaluators across dialogue, summarization, and question‑generation tasks.

findings

AdvEval dramatically lowers the performance of a wide range of NLG evaluators, achieving attack success rates up to 84.8% for high‑quality (R⁺) and 92.5% for low‑quality (R⁻) attacks in dialogue, similarly high rates in question evaluation, and strong improvements over both rule‑based and optimization‑based baselines in summarization. The gold LLM evaluator serves as a reliable human proxy (≈80% agreement with manual labels). The study shows that current automatic metrics are highly vulnerable to adversarial perturbations, that using LLM‑driven generation expands the search space and yields higher‑quality adversarial samples, and that removing the gold evaluator markedly degrades attack effectiveness. Overall, AdvEval provides an effective, scalable method for exposing and quantifying weaknesses in NLG evaluators.

5. LLM Comparative Assessment: Zero-shot NLG Evaluation through Pairwise Comparisons using Large Language Models
gpt-oss-120b
goal

To investigate and demonstrate the effectiveness of using large language models (LLMs) for zero‑shot natural language generation (NLG) evaluation via pairwise comparative assessment, analyzing its performance relative to absolute (prompt‑scoring) methods, examining positional biases, and exploring efficient ranking and debiasing techniques.

findings

Comparative assessment markedly outperforms absolute prompt‑scoring for moderate‑sized open‑source LLMs (FlanT5, Llama2‑chat), achieving near‑state‑of‑the‑art correlations across diverse NLG tasks (summarization, dialogue, data‑to‑text) while requiring minimal prompt engineering. LLMs exhibit strong positional bias in pairwise judgments; a simple debiasing scheme reduces this bias and yields notable performance gains, especially for highly biased systems. Efficient ranking is possible using subsets of comparisons (random, no‑repeat, symmetric) with only modest drops in Spearman correlation, and debiasing further mitigates the impact of reduced comparison sets. Overall, pairwise comparative assessment provides a simple, general, and effective zero‑shot NLG evaluation approach.
