1. On the Blind Spots of Model-Based Evaluation Metrics for Text Generation
gpt-oss-120b
goal

To investigate the robustness of recent pretrained‑language‑model‑based text‑generation evaluation metrics by designing synthetic stress‑test datasets that introduce controlled errors, and to expose and analyze the blind spots, biases, and failure modes of these metrics across open‑ended generation, translation, and summarization tasks.

findings

The study reveals multiple systematic weaknesses in PLM‑based metrics: BERTScore is misled by truncation errors in summarization; MAUVE (especially with GPT‑2 features) is insensitive to errors at the start or middle of generations and to sentence‑ordering disruptions, while MAUVE‑ELECTRA is more robust; GPT‑PPL and MLM‑PPL overly reward sequences composed of frequent n‑grams, showing a bias toward high‑frequency local patterns; self‑evaluation bias causes log‑probability metrics (GPT‑PPL, BARTScore) to favor outputs from the same underlying PLM used for evaluation; many metrics (various BERTScore, BARTScore, ROUGE, COMET, PRISM, UniEval) fail truncation tests, and some (BARTScore‑para‑recall, MAUVE‑RoBERTa) fail sentence‑switching tests; overall, PLM‑based metrics can be brittle, manipulable, and exhibit insensitivities that can be mitigated with alternative PLM features, metric combinations, and calibrated scoring.

The findings suggest that practitioners should use metric combinations to cover blind spots and consider alternative features (e.g., ELECTRA for MAUVE) for improved robustness.

2. Finding Blind Spots in Evaluator LLMs with Interpretable Checklists
gpt-oss-120b
goal

To introduce FBI, a meta-evaluation framework that uses interpretable checklists and targeted perturbations to assess how well evaluator LLMs can detect drops in factual accuracy, instruction following, long-form coherence, and reasoning ability of other LLMs.

findings

Current evaluator LLMs are unreliable: across 2400 perturbed answers in 22 categories, they miss quality degradations in over 50% of cases on average. Single‑answer and pairwise evaluations perform particularly poorly, while reference‑based evaluation is slightly better but still error‑prone. Even the strongest models (e.g., GPT‑4‑Turbo) fail to consistently penalize perturbed answers, explanations add only marginal benefit, and expanding the scoring range yields only modest gains. Overall, evaluator LLMs exhibit significant blind spots and should be used cautiously.

3. Order in the Evaluation Court: A Critical Analysis of NLG Evaluation Trends
gpt-oss-120b
goal

To systematically analyze the evolution of NLG evaluation practices over the past six years by automatically extracting metadata from 14,171 papers across major conferences, focusing on the use of automatic metrics, LLM-as-a-judge (LaaJ) methods, and human evaluation, and to derive actionable recommendations for improving evaluation rigor.

findings

1) Task divergence: Dialogue Generation has rapidly shifted toward LaaJ (>40% of papers by 2025), while Machine Translation remains dominated by n‑gram metrics and Question Answering shows a marked decline in human evaluation. 2) Metric inertia: Traditional generic metrics such as BLEU and ROUGE continue to be widely used across tasks despite the availability of more semantically informed metrics, and they often lack discriminative power for specific quality criteria. 3) Human‑LaaJ divergence: LaaJ and human evaluations prioritize different quality signals; explicit validation of LaaJ against humans is scarce (less than 8% of papers compare them) and reported correlations are only moderate to low, indicating that LaaJ is not a direct proxy for human judgment. 4) Validation gap: The exponential rise of LaaJ since 2023 is not matched by corresponding human validation, leading to a fragmented evaluation landscape.

4. Unveiling the Achilles' Heel of NLG Evaluators: A Unified Adversarial Framework Driven by Large Language Models
gpt-oss-120b
goal

To introduce AdvEval, a novel black‑box adversarial framework that uses large language models as both data generators and gold evaluators to automatically create adversarial NLG samples that cause strong disagreement between human (proxy) judgments and existing automatic evaluation metrics, thereby assessing and exposing the robustness weaknesses of NLG evaluators.

findings

AdvEval markedly outperforms rule‑based and optimization‑based baselines, achieving high attack success rates (e.g., up to 84.8% for positive‑label attacks and 92.5% for negative‑label attacks in dialogue evaluation) across 12 victim metrics and 11 datasets covering dialogue, summarization, and question generation. All tested metrics are vulnerable, with R‑ attacks generally easier than R+. The LLM gold evaluator serves as a reliable human proxy (≈80% accuracy vs. human labels). Ablation studies confirm that removing the gold evaluator sharply reduces performance, and the framework generates high‑quality, diverse adversarial texts while preserving label validity.

5. LLM Comparative Assessment: Zero-shot NLG Evaluation through Pairwise Comparisons using Large Language Models
gpt-oss-120b
goal

To investigate the use of large language models (LLMs) for zero‑shot natural language generation (NLG) evaluation via pairwise comparative assessment, assess its effectiveness relative to absolute prompt‑scoring, study positional biases in pairwise judgments, and explore efficient ranking strategies and debiasing techniques.

findings

Comparative assessment dramatically outperforms absolute prompt‑scoring for moderate‑sized open‑source LLMs (FlanT5, Llama2‑chat), achieving Spearman correlations comparable to or exceeding state‑of‑the‑art zero‑shot and supervised baselines across summarization, podcast, dialogue, and data‑to‑text tasks. The method works well even with models under 1B parameters and scales modestly with size. LLMs exhibit strong positional bias in pairwise decisions, especially larger models; a simple re‑weighting debiasing method reduces this bias and yields further performance gains, notably for biased systems. Subset selection strategies (random, no‑repeat, symmetric) can reduce the O(N²) comparison cost with minimal loss in correlation, and debiasing is especially beneficial when few comparisons are used. Overall, pairwise comparative assessment is a simple, general, zero‑shot approach that provides robust and near‑state‑of‑the‑art NLG evaluation.
