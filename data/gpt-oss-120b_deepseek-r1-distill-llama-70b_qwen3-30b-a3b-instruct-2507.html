1. On the Blind Spots of Model-Based Evaluation Metrics for Text Generation
gpt-oss-120b
goal

To introduce and apply a suite of synthetic stress tests for robustness analysis of pretrained‑language‑model‑based evaluation metrics across open‑ended generation, translation, and summarization, in order to uncover and understand their blind spots.

findings

The stress tests reveal numerous systematic weaknesses in popular PLM‑based metrics: BERTScore fails under truncation errors in summarization; MAUVE (with GPT‑2 features) is insensitive to errors at the start or middle of texts, while MAUVE‑ELECTRA is more robust; GPT‑PPL and MLM‑PPL are biased toward frequent n‑grams and can assign higher scores to artificially repetitive sequences; self‑evaluation bias causes log‑probability metrics (GPT‑PPL, BARTScore) to favor systems that share the same underlying PLM; several metrics (BARTScore variants, BERTScore, ROUGE, COMET, PRISM, UniEval, MAUVE‑RoBERTa) fail truncation tests, and BARTScore‑para‑recall and MAUVE‑RoBERTa fail sentence‑switching tests; injection tests show UniEval can be manipulated by valueless instruction text; overall, metric design choices and PLM properties lead to insensitivities, biases, and loopholes, prompting practical workarounds such as using alternative PLM features, reporting precision/recall separately, and combining complementary metrics.

The goal of this paper is to develop a methodology for robustness analysis of model-based evaluation metrics in text generation using stress tests with synthetic data. The authors aim to identify blind spots, biases, and potential loopholes in recently proposed pretrained language model (PLM)-based metrics across tasks such as open-ended generation, translation, and summarization by systematically inducing various types of errors and evaluating the resulting metric score drops.

findings

The paper reveals several critical blind spots in existing PLM-based metrics: BERTScore is insensitive to truncation errors in summarization, MAUVE (especially with GPT-2 features) fails to detect errors at the beginning or middle of generations due to limited long-range context encoding, and MAUVE is vulnerable to valueless injection text that tricks the metric into giving high scores. Additionally, log-probability-based metrics like GPT-PPL and BARTScore exhibit self-evaluation bias when the generator and evaluator are based on the same PLM. Frequent n-gram sequences, which lack global coherence, are incorrectly scored higher by GPT-PPL and MLM-PPL. The study also finds that some metrics fail to penalize sentence switching and truncation, indicating insensitivity to logical and informational consistency. The authors suggest using combinations of metrics, selecting better PLM features (e.g., ELECTRA over GPT-2 for MAUVE), and complementing log-probability metrics with reference-based or diversity metrics for more reliable evaluation.

2. Finding Blind Spots in Evaluator LLMs with Interpretable Checklists
gpt-oss-120b
goal

To develop and employ the FBI framework—a meta‑evaluation checklist using targeted perturbations—to systematically assess how well evaluator LLMs can detect quality degradations in factual accuracy, instruction following, long‑form coherence, and reasoning of other LLMs across single‑answer, pairwise, and reference‑guided evaluation strategies.

findings

Current evaluator LLMs perform poorly: they fail to identify quality drops in over 50% of perturbed cases on average. Single‑answer and pairwise evaluations show notable limitations, while reference‑based evaluation is relatively more reliable but still imperfect. GPT‑4‑Turbo, despite being the strongest, still misses many errors except in reasoning tasks; other models (Gemini‑1.5‑PRO, Claude‑3‑Opus, Llama‑3‑70B‑Instruct, Prometheus) exhibit similar or worse shortcomings. Adding rubrics or axes does not consistently improve detection, and expanding scoring ranges offers only marginal gains.

3. Order in the Evaluation Court: A Critical Analysis of NLG Evaluation Trends
gpt-oss-120b
goal

To systematically analyze how evaluation practices in Natural Language Generation have evolved over the past six years by automatically extracting and quantifying evaluation methods (automatic metrics, LLM-as-judge, and human evaluation) from 14,171 papers across major NLG conferences.

findings

The study uncovers (1) task‑specific shifts: Dialogue Generation rapidly adopts LaaJ (>40% in 2025) while Machine Translation remains tied to n‑gram metrics and Question Answering shows a steep drop in human evaluation; (2) metric inertia: generic metrics such as BLEU and ROUGE dominate across tasks despite the availability of more semantically aware metrics, often without empirical justification; (3) a validation gap: only a small minority of papers (<8%) directly compare LaaJ to human judgments, and the reported correlations are moderate to low, indicating that LaaJ and human evaluations prioritize different quality signals; (4) overall, LaaJ adoption has surged since 2023 but is rarely corroborated by human studies, highlighting a disconnect between automated and human evaluation methodologies.

4. Unveiling the Achilles' Heel of NLG Evaluators: A Unified Adversarial Framework Driven by Large Language Models
gpt-oss-120b
goal

To propose AdvEval, a unified black‑box adversarial framework that uses large language models as both data generators and gold evaluators to automatically craft high‑quality adversarial NLG samples that induce strong disagreement between human judgments and existing automatic NLG evaluators, thereby revealing and quantifying the robustness weaknesses of these evaluators across multiple tasks.

findings

AdvEval substantially outperforms rule‑based and optimization‑based baselines on 11 datasets covering dialogue, summarization, and question generation, achieving high attack success rates (e.g., 84.8% R+ and 92.5% R‑ for dialogue, 85% R+ and 58.8% R‑ for summarization). All 12 victim metrics are vulnerable, with R‑ attacks generally easier than R+. The LLM‑based gold evaluator serves as a reliable human proxy (over 80% agreement with manual labels). The framework’s larger search space and LLM‑driven feedback enable generation of diverse, high‑quality adversarial texts, confirming that current NLG evaluators lack robustness to such attacks.

5. LLM Comparative Assessment: Zero-shot NLG Evaluation through Pairwise Comparisons using Large Language Models
gpt-oss-120b
goal

To investigate the use of large language models (LLMs) for zero‑shot natural language generation (NLG) evaluation via pairwise (comparative) judgments, compare this approach to absolute score prediction, analyse positional biases in the comparisons, and explore efficient ranking strategies requiring fewer comparisons.

findings

Pairwise comparative assessment consistently outperforms absolute prompt‑scoring for moderate‑sized open‑source LLMs (FlanT5, Llama2‑chat), achieving Spearman correlations comparable to or better than state‑of‑the‑art and ChatGPT‑based methods across summarization, dialogue, podcast and data‑to‑text tasks. The method works even for sub‑1B models and shows diminishing returns after ~3B parameters. LLMs exhibit strong positional bias (favoring one position), especially in larger models; a simple re‑weighting debiasing technique reduces this bias and yields further performance gains, notably for biased systems. Efficient ranking with a subset of comparisons (random, no‑repeat, symmetric) retains most of the performance, and debiasing is especially beneficial when few comparisons are used. Overall, comparative assessment is a simple, general, zero‑shot NLG evaluation approach that surpasses absolute scoring and approaches or exceeds existing zero‑shot and supervised baselines.
