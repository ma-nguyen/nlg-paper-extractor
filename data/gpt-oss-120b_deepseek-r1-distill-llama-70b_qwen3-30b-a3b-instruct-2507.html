1. On the Blind Spots of Model-Based Evaluation Metrics for Text Generation
gpt-oss-120b
goal

To investigate the robustness of recent pretrained‑language‑model‑based evaluation metrics for text generation by designing synthetic stress‑test datasets that introduce controlled errors, and to analyze how these metrics respond to such perturbations across open‑ended generation, translation, and summarization tasks.

findings

The study uncovers systematic blind spots in many PLM‑based metrics: BERTScore is misled by truncation errors in summarization; MAUVE (especially with GPT‑2 features) is insensitive to errors at the beginning or middle of texts, while MAUVE‑ELECTRA is more robust; UniEval can be tricked by valueless injected instruction text; GPT‑PPL and MLM‑PPL unfairly favor sequences composed of frequent n‑grams; log‑probability metrics exhibit a self‑evaluation bias, rewarding outputs generated by the same underlying PLM used for evaluation; several metrics (e.g., BARTScore, BERTScore, ROUGE, COMET, PRISM, UniEval) fail truncation tests, and some (BARTScore‑para‑recall, MAUVE‑RoBERTa) fail sentence‑switching tests. The paper also provides analyses of why these failures occur (e.g., attention patterns, precision/recall trade‑offs) and suggests practical workarounds such as using alternative PLM features (e.g., RoBERTa/ELECTRA for MAUVE), reporting multiple score variants, calibrating f‑measure, and combining complementary metrics to mitigate individual blind spots.

goal

The goal of this paper is to evaluate the robustness of model-based evaluation metrics for text generation through systematic stress tests using synthetic data, focusing on identifying their blind spots and vulnerabilities.

findings

The findings reveal significant insensitivities, biases, and loopholes in various metrics. For instance, BERTScore struggles with truncation errors in summarization, MAUVE is insensitive to errors in certain positions, and some metrics show biases towards frequent n-grams or self-evaluation biases. Additionally, metrics like UniEval can be misled by injected misleading text, highlighting their vulnerability to manipulation.

goal

The goal of this paper is to develop a methodology for robustness analysis of model-based evaluation metrics in text generation using stress tests with synthetic data. The authors aim to uncover blind spots, biases, and loopholes in recently proposed pretrained language model (PLM)-based metrics across tasks such as open-ended generation, translation, and summarization, by systematically introducing synthetic errors and measuring the resulting metric score drops.

findings

The paper reveals several critical blind spots in popular PLM-based metrics: BERTScore is insensitive to truncation errors in summarization due to increasing precision canceling out decreasing recall; MAUVE (especially with GPT-2 features) is insensitive to errors at the beginning or middle of generations due to limited long-range context encoding; MAUVE is also vulnerable to valueless injection text that misleads its judgment; GPT-PPL and MLM-PPL are biased toward frequent n-grams rather than global coherence; log-probability-based metrics exhibit self-evaluation bias when the generator and evaluator share the same PLM; and several metrics fail to detect sentence switching, indicating insensitivity to logical and temporal order. The authors suggest using metric combinations and alternative PLM features (e.g., ELECTRA for MAUVE) to mitigate these issues.
