1. On the Blind Spots of Model-Based Evaluation Metrics for Text Generation
gpt-oss-120b
goal

To introduce a systematic suite of synthetic stress tests for evaluating the robustness of pretrained‑language‑model‑based metrics in natural‑language generation, uncover their blind spots, and propose practical mitigations.

findings

The stress tests reveal widespread insensitivities and biases in current PLM‑based metrics. BERTScore drops sharply with truncation errors in summarization, while MAUVE using GPT‑2 features is blind to errors at the start or middle of texts; switching to MLM‑based features (RoBERTa, ELECTRA) improves sensitivity. Several metrics (e.g., MAUVE‑GPT2, BERTScore, ROUGE, COMET, PRISM, UniEval, BARTScore) fail truncation tests, and BARTScore‑para‑recall and MAUVE‑RoBERTa fail sentence‑switching tests. Injection of valueless instruction text can mislead UniEval. GPT‑PPL and MLM‑PPL erroneously reward synthetic sequences composed of frequent n‑grams, showing a frequency bias. Log‑probability metrics exhibit self‑evaluation bias, favoring generations from the same underlying PLM used for evaluation (GPT‑2, BART, T5). Overall, the analyses demonstrate that PLM‑based metrics are brittle in many realistic error scenarios, and suggest using alternative feature encoders, calibrating precision/recall balances, avoiding metric‑generator PLM overlap, and combining complementary metrics to mitigate blind spots.

goal

To systematically analyze the robustness of model-based evaluation metrics for text generation by designing and applying stress tests with synthetic data, uncovering blind spots, biases, and insensitivities in existing metrics, and providing practical workarounds for more reliable evaluation.

findings

The paper reveals several blind spots in popular text generation evaluation metrics: (1) BERTScore is insensitive to truncation errors in summarization, often failing to penalize lost information; (2) MAUVE, particularly when using GPT-2 features, ignores errors at the beginning or middle of generated text due to its reliance on local context; (3) UniEval can be misled by valueless but contextually manipulative text (e.g., injection attacks); (4) Log-likelihood-based metrics like GPT-PPL and MLM-PPL favor frequent n-grams over coherent text, exposing biases in their scoring; (5) Metrics exhibit self-evaluation bias, unfairly favoring outputs from models matching their underlying architecture (e.g., GPT-PPL scores its own generations higher); (6) Metrics like BARTScore, BERTScore, and MAUVE fail to penalize sentence-switching errors, indicating insensitivity to temporal or logical disorder. The study also recommends practical fixes, such as using ELECTRA or RoBERTa features for MAUVE, reporting precision/recall separately for BERTScore, and combining metrics to mitigate individual blind spots.

2. Finding Blind Spots in Evaluator LLMs with Interpretable Checklists
gpt-oss-120b
goal

To introduce FBI, a meta-evaluation framework that uses interpretable checklists and targeted perturbations to systematically assess how well evaluator LLMs can detect quality degradations in other LLMs across four core abilities—factual accuracy, instruction following, long‑form coherence, and reasoning—and to benchmark various evaluator models under single‑answer, pairwise, and reference‑guided evaluation paradigms.

findings

Current evaluator LLMs are unreliable: across 2400 perturbed answers in 22 categories, they missed quality drops in over 50% of cases on average. Single‑answer and pairwise evaluations performed especially poorly, while reference‑based scoring was relatively better but still error‑prone. GPT‑4‑Turbo, though the strongest, failed to consistently penalize perturbed answers except on reasoning tasks, and advanced prompting strategies did not consistently improve performance. Other popular evaluators (Gemini‑1.5‑PRO, Claude‑3‑OPUS, LLAMA‑3‑70B‑INSTRUCT, PROMETHEUS) showed similar or limited gains, with LLAMA‑3 being overly strict in reference settings. Explanations offered only marginal benefit, and expanding the scoring range to 1‑5 yielded modest improvements.

3. Order in the Evaluation Court: A Critical Analysis of NLG Evaluation Trends
gpt-oss-120b
goal

To systematically analyze how evaluation practices in Natural Language Generation have evolved over the past six years by automatically extracting metadata from 14,171 conference papers, focusing on the use of automatic metrics, LLM-as-a-judge (LaaJ) methods, and human evaluation, and to derive actionable recommendations for improving NLG evaluation rigor.

findings

The study uncovers three major trends: (1) Task divergence – Dialogue Generation has rapidly shifted toward LaaJ (>40% of papers by 2025) while Machine Translation remains dominated by n‑gram metrics and Question Answering shows a steep decline in human evaluation; (2) Metric inertia – generic metrics such as BLEU and ROUGE continue to be widely used across tasks despite the availability of more semantically informed metrics, often without empirical justification; (3) Human‑LaaJ divergence – LaaJ and human evaluations prioritize different quality signals, show near‑zero correlation (Spearman ρ≈0.007), and explicit validation is rare (fewer than 8% of papers compare them, with only moderate to low correlation scores). These observations highlight a validation gap and motivate recommendations to modernize metric use, formalize metric‑criterion mappings, and adopt a stratified evaluation protocol that leverages LaaJ for technical checks and human judgment for high‑level dimensions.

4. Unveiling the Achilles' Heel of NLG Evaluators: A Unified Adversarial Framework Driven by Large Language Models
gpt-oss-120b
goal

To introduce AdvEval, a unified black‑box adversarial framework that automatically generates high‑quality NLG outputs causing strong disagreement between human (proxy) judgments and existing automatic evaluators, thereby assessing and exposing the robustness weaknesses of a wide range of NLG evaluation metrics across dialogue, summarization, and question‑generation tasks.

findings

Extensive experiments on 12 victim evaluators and 11 datasets show that most current NLG metrics are vulnerable to adversarial perturbations; AdvEval achieves markedly higher attack success rates than rule‑based and optimization‑based baselines (e.g., up to 84.8% R⁺ and 92.5% R⁻ in dialogue), can generate diverse, label‑preserving adversarial samples, and the LLM‑based gold evaluator serves as a reliable human proxy (≈80% agreement). Ablation studies confirm the critical role of the gold evaluator and evaluation criteria in guiding effective attacks.

goal

To introduce AdvEval, a novel black-box adversarial framework designed to automatically identify and exploit critical weaknesses in well-established natural language generation (NLG) evaluators by generating high-quality adversarial data that lead to significant disagreements between human judgment and victim evaluators, thereby assessing and highlighting the robustness vulnerabilities of various NLG evaluation metrics across multiple tasks including dialogue, summarization, and question evaluation.

findings

The paper finds that existing NLG evaluators, regardless of their type (reference-based, reference-free, or LLM-based), are highly vulnerable to adversarial perturbations. Specifically: (1) Traditional rule-based adversarial methods struggle to generate high-quality positive responses (R+) but are somewhat effective in creating negative responses (R-); (2) Optimization-based baseline methods (e.g., TextFooler, BERT-Attack) often fail to preserve the intended quality label, especially for R+, due to limited search space and inadequate quality constraints; (3) AdvEval significantly outperforms all baseline methods, achieving high attack success rates (ASR) across all tasks—84.8% for R+ and 92.5% for R- in dialogue evaluation, 60.6% for R+ and 92.7% for R- in question evaluation, and 85% for R+ and 58.8% for R- in summarization evaluation—demonstrating its ability to generate diverse, high-quality adversarial samples; (4) Manual inspection confirms that AdvEval maintains label validity in over 80% of cases, validating its use of LLMs as reliable proxies for human judgment; (5) Adversarial learning and ablation studies further underscore the effectiveness of AdvEval’s iterative optimization and dual-evaluator feedback mechanism.

5. LLM Comparative Assessment: Zero-shot NLG Evaluation through Pairwise Comparisons using Large Language Models
gpt-oss-120b
goal

To investigate and demonstrate the effectiveness of zero‑shot pairwise comparative assessment using large language models as an alternative to absolute scoring for evaluating natural‑language generation across various tasks, including analysis of performance, positional bias, and efficient ranking strategies.

findings

Comparative assessment consistently outperforms prompt‑scoring for moderate‑sized open‑source LLMs (FlanT5, Llama2‑chat), achieving near or state‑of‑the‑art correlations on summarization, dialogue, and data‑to‑text benchmarks; LLMs exhibit strong positional biases in pairwise judgments, which can be mitigated with a simple debiasing technique that yields further performance gains; efficient ranking is possible using subsets of comparisons (random, no‑repeat, symmetric) with only modest loss in correlation, and debiasing is especially beneficial when the number of comparisons is limited.

goal

To explore and demonstrate the effectiveness of using large language models (LLMs) for zero-shot natural language generation (NLG) evaluation through pairwise comparative assessment, rather than absolute scoring, while addressing challenges such as positional biases and scalable ranking efficiencies.
